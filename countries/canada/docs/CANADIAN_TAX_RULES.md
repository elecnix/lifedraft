# Complete Guide to Canadian Taxable Income Types & Investment Rules

## Authoritative CRA Publications

This document catalogs ALL types of taxable income and investment rules directly from CRA publications for personal tax optimization, with specific focus on RRSP and RESP strategies for families with 2-3 children.

---

## 1. TAXABLE INCOME CATEGORIES

### Business & Self-Employment Income (T4002)
**Publication:** T4002 - Self-employed Business, Professional, Commission, Farming, and Fishing Income  
**URL:** https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4002.html

**Taxable Types:**
- Business income (sole proprietorship)
- Professional income
- Commission income
- Farming income
- Fishing income

**Deductions Available:**
- Business expenses (T2125)
- Capital Cost Allowance (CCA)
- Home office expenses
- Vehicle expenses

---

### Employment Income
**Publication:** T4036 - Employment Expenses  
**URL:** https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4036.html

**Taxable Components:**
- Salary/wages
- Bonuses
- Commissions
- Tips/gratuities
- Allowances (most are taxable)

**Non-Taxable or Partially Taxable:**
- Certain travel allowances
- Tool deductions (up to $1,000)
- Uniform/clothing deductions

---

### Investment Income

#### Interest Income
- Bank interest (T5)
- GICs
- Corporate bonds
- Canada Savings Bonds
- **Fully taxable at marginal rate**

#### Dividend Income
**Publication:** T4037 - Tax Treatment of Dividends  
**URL:** https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4037.html

**Types:**
- **Eligible dividends** (gross-up 38%, federal dividend tax credit)
- **Non-eligible dividends** (gross-up 15%, smaller credit)
- **Foreign dividends** (foreign tax credit available)

**Tax Optimization:**
- Dividends can be more tax-efficient in lower brackets
- Gross-up moves you to higher bracket but credit offsets

#### Capital Gains
**Publication:** T4037 - Capital Gains 2025  
**URL:** https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4037/capital-gains.html

**Rules:**
- **50% inclusion rate** (only half is taxable)
- **Adjusted Cost Base** (ACB) tracking required
- **Principal residence exemption** (one per family unit)
- Capital loss carryforward indefinitely

**Optimization Strategies:**
- Harvest losses to offset gains
- Time gain realization for lower-income years

---

### Rental Income
**Publication:** T4037 - Rental Income  
**URL:** https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/t4037.html

**Taxable:**
- Full rental income
- Security deposits (if kept)
- Late fees/penalties

**Deductible Expenses:**
- Mortgage interest
- Property taxes
- Utilities
- Repairs/maintenance
- Depreciation (CCA - limited for rental)

## 2. REGISTERED RETIREMENT SAVINGS PLAN (RRSP) RULES

### RRSP Contribution Limits
**Publication:** IC72-22R10 - Registered Retirement Savings Plans  
**URL:** https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/ic72-22.html

**2025 Limits:**
- **Maximum contribution:** $32,840 (18% of earned income, max $32,840)
- **Cumulative room:** Sum of 18% of earned income minus pension adjustments (PAs)

### Tax Treatment
- **Contributions:** Tax-deductible (defer tax to withdrawal)
- **Growth:** Tax-sheltered (no annual tax on gains/dividends)
- **Withdrawals:** Fully taxable as income (withholding tax applies)

### Withholding Tax Rates
| Withdrawal Amount | Withholding Rate |
|-------------------|------------------|
| Up to $5,000 | 10% |
| $5,001-$15,000 | 10% |
| $15,001-$50,000 | 20% |
| Over $50,000 | 30% |

### Spousal RRSP Strategy
**Key Rule:** 3-year attribution if spouse withdraws within 3 years of contribution

## 3. REGISTERED EDUCATION SAVINGS PLAN (RESP) RULES

### RESP Contribution Limits & Rules
**Publication:** RC4092 - Registered Education Savings Plans  
**URL:** https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4092.html

**2025 Limits:**
- **Lifetime contribution:** $50,000 per child
- **Annual CESG:** $2,500 = $500 (20% match), $500-$10,000 = $250 (10% match)

### Canada Education Savings Grant (CESG)
**Maximum Lifetime:** $7,200 per child

### Canada Education Savings Bond (CESB)
- For low-income families
- Up to $500/year per child

### Provincial Programs
| Province | Program | Max |
|----------|---------|-----|
| Quebec | QESI | $300/year |
| Saskatchewan | SAGES | $250/year |
| Ontario | OSAP | Varies |

### RESP Withdrawal Rules
**Earnings Taxation:**
- **Student in post-secondary:** Taxed at student's (usually low) rate
- **EAP (Educational Assistance Payment):** 100% taxable to student
- **Non-EAP (contributions):** Not taxable, returned tax-free

### Family Plan Strategy
- **One RESP can hold beneficiaries** (multiple children)
- **EAPs can be split** among children
- **CESG room is individual** per child

**Optimization for 2-3 Kids:**
- Contribute $2,500/child early for full CESG ($500/child)
- Family plan allows pooling contributions
- Catch-up contributions for older child if younger siblings have unused room

## 4. TAX OPTIMIZATION STRATEGIES

### Income Splitting Strategies

#### 1. Spousal RRSP
- Contribute to lower-income spouse's RRSP
- Withdraw during higher-income spouse's low-income years
- Avoid 3-year attribution rule

#### 2. RESP Family Plan
- Split CESG contributions between children
- Time EAP withdrawals with student's low-income years
- Use youngest child's later school start for catch-up

#### 3. Capital Gains Harvesting
- Trigger gains in low-income years
- Use capital losses first
- Consider principal residence exemption timing

### Tax Bracket Optimization

#### Marginal Rate Arbitrage
```
Strategy: Convert high-rate income to lower-rate income
- RRSP withdrawal in retirement (likely lower tax bracket)
- Capital gains (50% inclusion)
- Dividends in low bracket (gross-up + credit advantage)
```

## 6. RESP OPTIMIZATION SCENARIOS

### Family with 2 Children (Ages 5 and 8)

**Strategy:**
1. Contribute $2,500/child annually for CESG ($500 each)
2. Use family plan to pool unused CESG room
3. Catch-up: Younger child can use older sibling's unused years

**Numbers:**
- Annual contribution: $5,000 (2 children × $2,500)
- Annual CESG: $1,000 ($500 × 2)
- 18 years: $90,000 contribution, $36,000 CESG (full lifetime)

### Family with 3 Children (Ages 2, 5, 8)

**Strategy:**
1. Maximize CESG for youngest first
2. Use any catch-up room
3. Family plan allows flexibility

**Optimal Annual:**
- Year 1-16: $7,500 (3 × $2,500) = $1,500 CESG
- Year 17-18: $5,000 (oldest in school) = $1,000 CESG
